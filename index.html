Who this is for

You built your app with an AI builder, wrapped it for iOS (or exported an Expo project), and either just got a rejection you don't understand, or you're about to submit and don't want to lose two weeks to a reviewer's note. You can't read the native project yourself, and your builder's chat can't see what Apple sees: the wrapper configuration, the permission strings, the privacy manifest, the listing.

The 12 checks

Each finding is labelled pass warn fail or needs a human, with a confidence level. Guideline numbers refer to Apple's App Review Guidelines.

  1. Placeholder text and dead links — "Lorem ipsum", "TBD", "test", sample data, broken URLs in the app or the listing. (2.1)
  2. Reviewer can get in — if your app has a login, a working demo account is in the review notes; nothing is behind a paywall the reviewer can't pass. (2.1, 2.3.1)
  3. Listing matches the app — screenshots show the real app, the description doesn't promise features that aren't there, the age rating fits. (2.3)
  4. Privacy policy — the URL loads, names the data you collect and the third parties you share it with. (5.1.1)
  5. Permission purpose strings — every permission you request (camera, photos, location, microphone, contacts, tracking) explains specifically why, in your words, not boilerplate. Missing strings are rejected at upload. (5.1.1, ITMS-90683)
  6. Privacy manifest — required-reason APIs declared and manifests present for the SDKs Apple lists. Missing ones are rejected at upload. (ITMS-91053, ITMS-91061)
  7. Not just a website in a box — no live-site server.url in production, real native behaviour beyond what the browser already does. (4.2)
  8. Not a template clone — distinctive name, icon and features; a saturated-category check; review notes that say what's different. (4.3)
  9. Remote updates stay in bounds — over-the-air update tools (Expo Updates, Capgo, CodePush and builder-specific OTA) configured for fixes inside the sandbox, not for shipping features around review. (2.5.2, advisory)
  10. Sign in with Apple — present if you offer Google, Facebook or any third-party login. (4.8)
  11. Payments in the right place — digital goods and subscriptions through in-app purchase, not a web checkout inside the app; physical goods and services exempt. (3.1.1)
  12. Account deletion and build sanity — in-app account deletion if users can sign up; no "beta" or "test" labels; builds and runs on current iOS; iPad layout if you claim iPad. (5.1.1(v), 2.1, 2.4)

Honest answers

Will my app pass if I fix everything on the list?

We don't know, and nobody can promise that. Some rejections (4.2 "minimum functionality", 4.3 "spam") are judgement calls by a reviewer. We tell you which findings are mechanical and which are opinions, and we label our confidence on each. We never publish or claim a success rate.

What do you need from me?

A GitHub link or a zip of the project (no secrets, please; see privacy), your App Store listing text, and, if you have it, the full text of Apple's message. An .ipa file works too if that's all you have.

Is this legal or compliance advice?

No. It is a technical review against Apple's published guidelines. For legal questions about your privacy policy or terms, talk to a lawyer.

Which builders do you cover?

Web apps from Lovable, Bolt, Base44 and similar tools that were wrapped with Capacitor; Expo and React Native projects, including Replit and a0.dev exports; plain Xcode projects. If your builder already includes its own App Store reviewer (Rork, Vibecode), use that first.
